# Accuracy rules — read before writing a single word of page copy

This page carries my name and my employer's. Everything on it has to survive a former
colleague reading it. These rules are lifted from the CV and cover-letter rules I already
maintain privately, narrowed to what a public page needs.

## 1. Never invent a number

No metric appears on this page unless it appears in [about.md](about.md) or
[projects.md](projects.md). Invented traction numbers, "trusted by", "10× faster" and round
percentages nobody measured are the fastest way to make the whole page untrustworthy.

If a layout wants a number that doesn't exist, change the layout.

## 2. No employers, no titles, no dates, no employer metrics

**The page carries what doesn't change. LinkedIn carries what does.**

Never on this page:

- **Company names**, past or present, mine or anyone's. Not in the bio, not in a timeline, not
  in a footnote.
- **A job title tied to a company**, and any claim about where I work now. Employment moves;
  a public page written in the present tense goes wrong quietly and stays wrong.
- **Dates and durations tied to a role.** "Nine years in product" is fine. "2021 to 2024
  at X" is not.
- **Employer metrics.** The CV carries real adoption numbers from real jobs. They belong in an
  application sent to one company, under that company's name. On a public page they are
  someone else's numbers.
- **Internal scale numbers** of any kind: engineer counts, service counts, repo counts,
  component counts.

Instead, the page says what I work on and how I think about it, then hands off:

> LinkedIn has the current version of the working history.

This also means the page needs no maintenance when a job changes. That's the point.

## 3. Ownership, said at the right size

The bio describes areas of work, not achievements, so most of the old CV rules don't apply
here. Two that still do:

- **Never claim to own a whole platform.** I work on the surfaces engineers touch. Say that.
- **Banned verbs**: "architected", "orchestrated", "spearheaded", and any solo verb on work a
  team did.

The test: would a former colleague nod, or squint?

## 4. Side projects are side projects

Say so. They're evening work, most of them early. "Actively developed" and "early" are more
credible than polish claims, and they're true.

## 5. Voice

I'm fluent but not a native English speaker, and the page should sound like me rather than
like a model.

Banned patterns, all of which I already reject in my own writing:

- **"Not X, but Y."** Cut every instance. It's the most reliable tell there is.
- **Italic emphasis inside a heading.** Carry emphasis with weight or colour.
- **Wind-up openers**: "Something I've noticed…", "One thing I've learned…", "In today's world…".
- **Paragraph wrap-up sentences** that restate what the paragraph just proved.
- **Forced rule-of-three** and perfectly balanced parallel constructions.
- **Clichés**: "passionate about", "seamless", "robust", "leveraged", "simply", "just".
- **Em-dash continuation** ("— building X, enabling Y"). Use commas or a full stop.

Prefer: short sentences mixed with longer ones. Direct statements. Contractions.

## 6. Personal data — LinkedIn and GitHub, nothing else

On the page: a LinkedIn link and a GitHub link.

Never on the page: **email address** (including a `mailto:` behind a "get in touch" button, an
obfuscated address, or a contact form), **phone number**, home address, city, anything about
my family, and anything about citizenship, relocation or language study.

Anyone who wants to reach me goes through LinkedIn. A design that wants a contact section
builds it from two links.

## 7. When a fact needs checking

The sources, in order:

1. `content/about.md` and `content/projects.md` in this repo. Start here.
2. Each project's own README, plus `gh repo view batirko/<name>`
3. My private profile file — the full honesty rules this file summarises. Ask me for it.

**Do not source from my CV.** It is stale on current employment, and everything it is good for
— employers, titles, dates, metrics — is banned from this page by rule 2 anyway.

If nothing settles it, leave it out and ask me.
